The board position · what the church owns and what it contributes

The board position.

The church is not asking a developer for a favour. It is contributing land worth $4m to $15m and receiving in return a freehold precinct (childcare centre, 500-seat auditorium and café) worth $38m to $45m capitalised, generating $2.47m/year from day one of stabilisation, growing to $3.66m/year by year 10. The church’s $4m to $6m building contribution is recovered from precinct income within 2 to 4 years. After that, the income is entirely free, funding church programs, mission and growth for generations.

The single lever · the return on the church’s investment

The single lever, and the return.

The single lever

~$4.5madded to land value by every $50k rise in the average sale price
~6%added to developer margin by every $50k rise

The land has no residual value at $900k. The developer can’t be paid for their risk. At $1.05m the church’s land is worth ~$13.4m and the project becomes fundable. This is the single lever the board needs to understand.

The return on the church’s investment

$55m to $70mcompleted freehold precinct, gross
4 to 5xreturn on total investment

Even in the worst case (church contributes $6m cash plus land worth $4m) the church ends up with a completed freehold precinct worth $55m to $70m gross. That is a 4 to 5x return on their total investment, paid for almost entirely by the developer through the residential development. The land never leaves church ownership. The building is delivered debt-free. The income stream starts immediately. This is what ‘land as equity’ looks like in practice.
